# Simulation Models

Scientific approach per engine module and how results reach the screen. Each implementation cites its sources in code; this file is the overview. Rows marked **(built)** are implemented and tested; the others are the plan.

## Grid

Marmara bounding box 26.0–31.0° E, 39.8–41.8° N. Engine grid `marmara-1km`: 0.01° cells, 500 × 200 (100,000 cells); phones may downsample by 2. Tsunami grid `marmara-500m`: 0.005°, 1000 × 400. Grids are north-up (row 0 = 41.8° N), row-major, little-endian `Float32Array`, NaN for no data; specs live in `manifest.json`, `src/types/grid.ts` is the shared type and `src/data/grid.ts` decodes them. Prepared inputs on these grids: population, Vs30, elevation, district index (1 km), bathymetry (500 m). **Event grid (since 2026-10-27):** shaking (arrivals, PGA, PGV, MMI) is computed on an event-centred grid, `engine/grid/event-grid.ts`.
- Size: the rupture's surface projection widened on every side by the distance where the ASB14 median on stiff soil (Vs30 360 m/s) falls to MMI III (`EDGE_MMI`, the lowest intensity the map draws).
- Radii: Mw 6 → 142 km, Mw 7 → 408 km, Mw 7.4 → 612 km, Mw 7.8 → 990 km (capped at 1,500 km).
- Cells: square, about 250k on desktops and 100k on phones, never finer than 1 km. Mw 7.4 gets about 1.7 km cells, Mw 7.8 about 4 km. Travel-time tables reach the farthest grid corner.
- Vs30 inside the regional grid comes from the data; outside it is 760 m/s (`DEFAULT_VS30`).
- The regional grid (`marmara-1km`) still carries the impact modules. Districts, damage, network and routing are computed on a second shaking pass over it, only when the event grid overlaps it.
- Stations take Vs30 from the regional data or the default, wherever they are.
- Caveat: ASB14 has no anelastic term, so far-field intensities (beyond its 200 km range) are extrapolated and probably high.

## Modules

| Module | Approach |
|---|---|
| `velocity` **(built)** | IASP91 crust and uppermost mantle (Kennett and Engdahl, 1991): 0–20 km Vp 5.80 / Vs 3.36, 20–35 km Vp 6.50 / Vs 3.75, mantle Vp 8.04 / Vs 4.47 km/s, verified against ObsPy's `iasp91.tvel`. First arrival = min(direct wave by ray-parameter bisection, head waves on every deeper interface). `TravelTimeCurve` tabulates first arrivals against distance (1 km steps, to 700 km) for each subfault depth. A regional Marmara model can replace IASP91 without code changes. |
| `rupture` **(built)** | Planar finite fault below the ruptured part of the trace (right-hand rule: dip direction = strike + 90°), width = seismogenic thickness / sin(dip). Subfaults: the hypocentre itself (radiating at t = 0) plus a grid of about 4 km along strike × 3 rows down dip. Circular rupture front from the hypocentre on the fault plane at 0.8 × Vs at the hypocentre (Heaton, 1990). Mw from rupture area with Wells and Coppersmith (1994) Table 2A (`M = 3.98 + 1.02·log A` for strike-slip), unless the scenario sets it. Leonard (2014) was the original plan; W&C94 is used because its coefficients were verified against OpenQuake's `WC1994`. |
| `sim` **(built)** | Arrival per cell = min over subfaults of (front time + first-arrival time at the chord distance). Ground motion = ASB14 median at the cell's Rjb (distance to the rupture's surface projection) and Vs30; MMI from PGA and PGV. Also returns per-district first P/S arrivals and peak MMI, the rupture summary, station records and the scenario duration (the later of the last S arrival on the grid and the end of every station's S window, + 5 s). About 0.5–0.9 s per preset in Node, of which the grid is 0.2–0.4 s; runs in a Web Worker in the app. |
| `gmpe` **(built)** | Akkar, Sandıkkaya and Bommer (2014), Rjb model: PGA, PGV, SA(0.3 s), SA(1.0 s); nonlinear Vs30 site term driven by the rock PGA; normal/reverse style terms from rake. Coefficients from OpenQuake hazardlib `AkkarEtAlRjb2014`; tests reproduce its 540-row verification table to 1e-6 in ln. Medians only in phase 2; aleatory variability (σ, τ available) comes with the random module in phase 3. |
| `intensity` **(built)** | Worden, Gerstenberger, Rhoades and Wald (2012) GMICE without magnitude/distance terms, with the ShakeMap low-amplitude extension (coefficients verified in USGS esi-shakelib `wgrw12.py`), g = 981 cm/s² as in ShakeMap. Combined MMI: PGA-based up to V, PGV-based from VII, linear weight between (Wald et al., 1999). Clamped to I–X. Bilal and Askan (2014) remains a candidate for a Türkiye-specific conversion once its coefficients are verified from the paper. |
| `random` **(built)** | mulberry32 uniform deviates, Box–Muller normals, FNV-1a sub-stream seeds per station id, so a station's record does not depend on the station list. `Scenario.seed` (presets: 1). |
| `synth` **(built)** | Stochastic method (Boore 2003): windowed Gaussian noise (SMSIM exponential window, ε 0.2, η 0.05, tη = 2 × duration; Boore 2002 OFR 00-509), normalized, multiplied by the point-source acceleration spectrum and inverted. S spectrum: Campbell (2003) WNA defaults as in pyrvt v0.8.1 (β 3.5 km/s, ρ 2.8, Δσ 100 bar, Q 180 f^0.45, κ 0.04, spreading R⁻¹/R⁻⁰·⁵ at 40 km, duration 1/f0 + 0.05 R, crustal amplification table), distance = nearest-subfault distance (≈ Rrup). P phase: (0.52/0.63)(β/α)³ (Boore & Boatwright 1984), corner × 0.32/0.21 (Madariaga 1976), α = √3 β, sharing the S attenuation (simplification). Components: P on Z (cos i) and radial (sin i, Snell's law with Vp = √3·Vs30); S independent on N and E, on Z at 2/3 (Newmark & Hall 1982). One scale factor sets the geometric mean of the horizontal peaks to the ASB14 median (OpenQuake `IMC.GEOMETRIC_MEAN`). Gaussian noise floor 0.05 cm/s² rms (assumption). 100 Hz, Z/N/E, 60 s of pre-event noise for the LTA. Accelerometer stations only (50). |
| `eew` **(built)** | Location: grid search (0.01°, ±0.5° around the stations) on the first four triggers, depth fixed at 10 km, origin time = mean residual. Magnitude: Pd modelled from the rupture grown in 3 s (disc of radius Vr·3 s, cut to the fault width; W&C94 area → M, capped at Mw) through Wu & Zhao (2006) log Pd = −3.463 + 0.729 M − 1.374 log R with σ 0.305 (seeded), inverted at the estimated distance and averaged — Pd saturates as in real systems. Alert = 4th trigger + 3 s (Pd window; Wu et al. 2007); no telemetry latency. Warning time per district = S arrival − alert; ≤ 0 is the blind zone. Presets: location error 1–2 km, first estimate M 6.2–6.5, alert T+6–11 s. Phase 3 part: classic STA/LTA on |a| of the vertical (Trnkoczy 2012: STA 0.5 s, LTA 60 s, trigger 4; ObsPy window semantics); first trigger per station, 0.01–0.7 s after the P arrival. **Planned:** STA/LTA triggering on synthetic records; grid-search location and Pd/τc magnitude estimate from the first four triggered stations; alert time T_alert. Warning time per district = S arrival − T_alert; negative values form the blind zone. |
| `damage` **(built)** | GEM Global Vulnerability Model v2026.0.0 (Türkiye) mean loss ratios, structural and fatality, instead of HAZUS fragility (verifiable, Türkiye-specific). Stock classes: period → code level (before 1980 CDL+ERL, 1980–2000 CDM+ERL, after 2000 CDM+ERM; a modelling judgement), storeys 1–4/5–9/9–19 → H:3/6/10, period and storeys independent. Buildings and residents spread over district cells by population. Each class evaluated at its own measure (PGA, SA 0.3/0.6/1.0 from ASB14, SA 0.6 added) and averaged over the ASB14 total σ (ε ∈ [−3, 3], 0.5 steps), because vulnerability is convex. Outputs per district: loss ratio, complete-loss-equivalent buildings, night fatalities (everyone home). Facilities (hospitals, fire, police): CDM+ERM/H:3/COM loss ratio; display states operational < 5 % ≤ limited < 20 % ≤ likely out (assumption). Validation: North Marmara vs İBB 2019 — district fatality rank correlation 0.77 (buildings 0.55), İstanbul fatalities 6,000 vs 14,145. |
| `grid` **(built)** | `settlement.ts`: settlement class per 1 km cell from population density with the Degree of Urbanisation level-1 thresholds (European Commission and Eurostat, 2021): urban centre ≥ 1,500 /km², urban cluster ≥ 300 /km², rural below (density only, no contiguity rules); cell areas on the sphere. `point-index.ts`: nearest-point queries on the grid (sites, road nodes). |
| `network` **(built)** | Cell sites: OpenCelliD when built, otherwise synthetic — each land cell gets the site density of a hexagonal macro layout at the ITU-R M.2135-1 (2009, Table 8-4) inter-site distance of its settlement class (urban macro 500 m, suburban 1,299 m, rural 1,732 m; site area (√3/2)·ISD²), scattered with a fixed seed: about 32,750 sites. Service areas: each land cell served by the nearest site (raster Voronoi), residents shared among the sites inside a cell; residents per km² per site. Demand relative to the busy hour (each site dimensioned for its own busy hour): peak 55× (voice traffic 50–60× normal after Tōhoku 2011, NTT DOCOMO Annual Report 2011) times the share who felt it (linear from MMI II to VI, USGS MMI descriptions; assumption), jump at the S arrival and exponential decay with e-folding 1.42 h / 3 (Bagrow et al. 2011, PLoS ONE: jump–decay, earthquake anomaly 1.42 h; shape assumption). Site down when its host building (GEM CDM+ERM/H:3/COM, the facility class) reaches 20 % expected loss; power cuts and batteries not modelled. Calls not carried at demand d: 1 − 1/d. North Marmara: all reached sites congested, about 98 % of call attempts not carried, no site down. |
| `routing` **(built)** | OpenStreetMap major roads (CSR graph). Free-flow speed from the car limits of the Karayolları Trafik Yönetmeliği Art. 100: motorway 120, trunk 110, other 90, 50 km/h in urban-centre cells (motorways exempt). Bridges: Hazus v5.1 HWB28 (unclassified bridge) fragility from the NHERI SimCenter DLML (`transportation_network/portfolio/Hazus v5.1/fragility.csv`, commit 192d280): Sa(1.0) medians 0.8/1.0/1.2/1.7 g, β 0.6; closed when the median Sa(1.0) reaches the extensive median 1.2 g (no skew/span modifiers). Debris (assumption): secondary/tertiary streets in urban-centre cells slowed to time/(1 − L), L the cell's expected building loss (cap 0.9), reported from L ≥ 5 %; arterials kept clear as emergency routes. A first version closed streets at L ≥ 20 % and fragmented İzmit into islands; replaced. Multi-source Dijkstra (binary heap) from fire stations before and after (stations ≥ 20 % loss removed), and from hospitals after. Districts reached at the road node nearest their population-weighted centre (reachable before; after: on an open road). Corridors: fire and hospital routes of every district with ≥ 1 lost building, weighted by lost buildings, per edge, normalised; shown from the district's S arrival. North Marmara: median fire response 5.8 → 5.9 min, largest delay Zeytinburnu +0.5 min, 2,650 km of streets slowed, no bridge closed; Adalar has no road link to a hospital. Whole scenario about 0.5 s. |
| `tsunami` **(built)** | Source: Okada (1985) vertical surface displacement (uz, Chinnery notation, μ/(λ+μ) = 0.5), ported from GeoClaw `dtopotools.py` (commit 8155d73) and checked against Okada's Table 2 (via okada85_checklist.m, IPGP deformation-lib c45a5af); one rectangle per segment of the ruptured trace, uniform slip D = M0/(μA) with μ = 3×10¹⁰ Pa (W&C94) and the fault rake; uplift copied to the sea surface at t = 0 (instantaneous, no horizontal-motion or Kajiura terms). North Marmara: D 2.1 m, seafloor +0.30 / −0.55 m. Propagation: linear shallow-water equations, Arakawa C grid with volume fluxes, forward-backward (linear TUNAMI-N1, Imamura et al. 2006), on the 500 m EMODnet grid; land is a wall, depth floored at 10 m (no run-up), open grid edges with √(gh)·η radiation, dt from CFL (≈1.9 s). One hour in about 2.5–3 s in its own worker. Output: frames every 15 s averaged onto the 1 km grid (int16 mm, 15 MB), maximum surface and arrival (first 1 cm change from the initial surface) per sea cell, and per coastal district (sea cells next to land) first arrival and highest surface. North Marmara: first coast 0.5 min (Adalar), highest 1.8 m (Marmaraereğlisi), Black Sea coast sheltered; İzmit-like strongest inside İzmit Bay. Labeled educational: linear, no dispersion, no landslide sources (the 1999 İzmit Bay waves were partly landslide-driven). |
| `aftershocks` **(built)** | Reasenberg and Jones (1989, eq. 4) rate λ(t, M) = 10^(a + b(Mm − M))·(t + c)^−p with the generic California parameters a −1.67, b 0.91, p 1.08, c 0.05 day (USGS OFR 96-266). The fitted sequence includes secondary aftershocks, so it is sampled as one Poisson process (count from the closed-form integral, times by inverting the Omori CDF, magnitudes from truncated Gutenberg–Richter below the mainshock) instead of an ETAS cascade, which with these values would be near-critical. M ≥ 3 for 72 h, scenario seed sub-stream `aftershocks`. Locations: uniform along the ruptured trace and across the fault's surface projection, Gaussian scatter 5 km (assumption), uniform depth in the seismogenic layer. Hourly 24-hour probabilities of M ≥ 5 and M ≥ 6 from the same model. North Marmara: about 950 M ≥ 3 in three days (714 by 24 h), largest M 6.8. California-calibrated, not a Marmara fit; not an official forecast. |

## Validation against observations

Median PGA (ASB14 Rjb with the USGS Vs30 site term, rupture placed by `scenarioFromEvent`) versus PGA recorded at KOERI strong-motion stations (KO network, HN? channels, flat sensitivity, geometric mean of the horizontal peaks; `tests/fixtures/validation/`):

| Event | Stations | Mean ln(obs/model) | σ of ln residuals |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 Silivri, Mw 5.7 (USGS us70005lkl) | 12 (Rjb 53–188 km) | +0.10 | 0.51 |
| 2025 Marmara Ereğlisi, Mw 6.2 (USGS us7000pufs) | 43 (Rjb 20–185 km) | +0.09 | 0.50 |

Both means are within the ±0.3 success criterion (`projectbrief.md`), tested in `tests/integration/engine/ground-motion-validation.test.ts`. Slightly positive means suggest the model underestimates these two events a little; single stations scatter by up to about ±1 in ln, which is ordinary for a GMPE median.

## Timeline

One global clock `t` in seconds from origin (`state/playback-store.ts`). Built: play/pause, slider, keyboard (Space, ←/→ step, Shift jump), event markers for the first detection (`wave-p`), the EEW alert (`critical`) and the tsunami's first coastal arrival (`tsunami-pos`). Built (phase 7–8): timeline scales — Shaking (0 to the scenario duration, speeds 0.5–10×, steps 1 / 10 s), Tsunami (0 to 60 min, speeds 10–120×, steps 10 / 60 s, available once the tsunami worker finishes) and Response (0 to 72 h, speeds 600–14,400×, steps 10 min / 1 h); marker for the largest aftershock. The clock reads h:mm:ss from an hour on. Planned: S arrival at central İstanbul marker.

Inserted below the reference lines and above the basemap roads. |

| P/S wavefronts **(built)** | Isoline of the arrival-time grid at `t` (marching squares), lifted onto the terrain with the elevation grid and repeated at 5 heights (250–2,850 m, fading) as a curtain of light: deck.gl `LineLayer` with binary attributes. P cyan (`--wave-p`), S amber (`--wave-s`). |

## Real earthquakes as scenarios (phase 9)

`features/scenario/lib/event-scenario.ts`: within 15 km of a trace the hypocentre goes to the point of the nearest EFSM20 trace (distance reported; 1.4 km for 2025, 9.7 km for 2019); farther away the rupture stays at the epicentre on a straight 120 km trace with the nearest fault's local strike, dip, rake and depths (an assumption for off-fault seismicity), at the catalog depth clamped to the seismogenic layer, with the catalog magnitude. The rupture spans a known extent (1999: the İzmit preset's 30.90–29.35° E) or the W&C94 median area for the magnitude divided by the fault width, centred on the hypocentre and kept on the trace (`engine/rupture/finite-fault.ts`, `ruptureExtentKm`). Live events are offered from M 4.0. Observed records replace synthetic ones in telemetry per station (components the station lacks stay flat); their STA/LTA pick is not shown.

## Presets

Built (`src/features/scenario/lib/presets.ts`), all on the Main Marmara Fault (EFSM20 `TRCF045`, one 520 km source digitized east to west, dip 74°, rake −157°, 0.5–18 km), extents given as longitudes along the trace:

| Preset | Extent | Hypocentre | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| North Marmara (Central Marmara – Princes' Islands) | 29.15° → 27.75° E | 28.25° E, 12 km | 121 km, Mw 7.4 |
| Kumburgaz segment | 28.95° → 28.45° E | 28.70° E, 12 km | about Mw 6.9 |
| Princes' Islands segment | 29.25° → 28.85° E | 29.05° E, 10 km | about Mw 6.8 |
| İzmit (1999-like) | 30.90° → 29.35° E | 29.90° E, 15 km | about Mw 7.4 (1999: Mw 7.6) |

Segment names follow the literature (Le Pichon et al., 2001; Armijo et al., 2002); limits are approximate and say so in the UI. Historical replays (built, phase 9; USGS ComCat parameters in `features/replay/lib/historical-events.ts`): 1999 Kocaeli Mw 7.6, 2019 Silivri Mw 5.7, 2025 Marmara Ereğlisi – Silivri Mw 6.2. 1912 Mürefte is not included: it predates instrumental Mw in the catalogs used.

## Known model limits

- Medians only: observed 1999 intensities (IX–X near İzmit Bay) exceed the median VIII; the spread is within σ.
- One fault plane per scenario; multi-fault ruptures (e.g. jumping to a parallel strand) are not modelled.
- Flat-layered velocity model without topography or basin effects; Vs30 from topographic slope.
